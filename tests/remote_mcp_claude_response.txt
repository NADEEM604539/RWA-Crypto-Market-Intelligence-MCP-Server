Tokenized gold layer (PAXG / broader GOLD RWA class)

The structural story here is counterintuitive: PAXG's turnover ratio (3.28%) actually exceeds Bitcoin's (1.26%), meaning relative to its size, tokenized gold sees more active trading velocity than BTC does — likely reflecting arbitrage flows keeping it pegged to spot gold rather than organic speculative demand. ETH's turnover (3.11%) is comparable to PAXG's, consistent with ETH's higher-beta, actively-traded profile.

Scale asymmetry: BTC's market cap is 860x larger than PAXG's, and ETH's is 169x larger. This isn't a liquidity-depth failure on gold's part — it reflects that tokenized RWA gold is still a nascent wrapper product around a trillion-dollar physical bullion market, whereas BTC/ETH are native, unwrapped crypto-native assets carrying the full weight of their asset class.

Macro dominance context
Total crypto market cap sits at $2.78T, with Bitcoin dominance at roughly 58.8% and Ethereum dominance at 11.6%. Against that backdrop, PAXG's $1.9B cap is a rounding error (~0.07% of total crypto market cap) — tokenized gold remains a niche instrument, not a competing store-of-value force to BTC within crypto capital allocation, though it serves a different structural role (dollar-stable, bullion-pegged collateral) than a speculative or monetary-premium asset like BTC.

Issuer/structural fragmentation risk
The RWA gold ecosystem is fragmented across at least 7 issuers (Paxos, Tether, Matrixdock, Comtech, VNX), while the broader tokenized RWA space spans 25 tracked issuers — Backed Assets alone accounts for 1,176 tokens, dwarfing the single-asset-focused gold issuers. This fragmentation means gold-token liquidity is split rather than pooled, unlike BTC/ETH which trade as single fungible assets across all venues — a structural liquidity disadvantage for any single gold token relative to crypto majors.

Bottom line: PAXG offers tight peg-tracking and decent relative turnover for its size, but sits in a fragmented, sub-scale niche (~0.07% of total crypto market cap) compared to BTC and ETH's deep, unified liquidity pools. It functions more as a stable collateral/hedge instrument than a liquid trading asset at institutional size.
